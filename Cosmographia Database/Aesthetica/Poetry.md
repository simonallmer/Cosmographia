# The Structuralist Definition of Poetry

The **Structuralist Definition of Poetry** is a formalist school of thought asserting that "Poetry" is a distinct category of literature defined exclusively by the presence of **meter** (rhythm) and/or **rhyme**. Under this definition, poetry is viewed as a "musical" construction of language where the artistic value is derived from the poet’s ability to navigate fixed constraints.

## 1. Core Philosophy

The fundamental tenet of this definition is that **form dictates identity**. Proponents argue that just as music requires melody or rhythm to be distinguished from ambient noise, poetry requires a consistent "beat" or "phonetic pattern" to be distinguished from speech.

> _"Writing free verse is like playing tennis with the net down."_ > — **Robert Frost** (A primary influence on this perspective)

## 2. Inclusion Criteria

To be classified as "Poetry" under this standard, a work must satisfy at least one of the following mechanical requirements:

- **Meter (Rhythmic Constraint):** The text must follow a repeatable, mathematical pattern of stressed and unstressed syllables (e.g., Iambic Pentameter). The rhythm must be "baked into the code" of the words so that it is audible even when read by a monotone voice.
    
- **Rhyme (Phonetic Constraint):** The use of matching terminal sounds (End Rhyme) or internal phonetic echoes that create a predictable structural skeleton.
    
- **Aural Independence:** The "poetic" nature of the work must be detectable by ear alone, without the need to see the visual layout on a page.
    

## 3. Exclusion Criteria (The "Lineated Prose" Category)

This definition explicitly excludes several modern forms that are often colloquially labeled as poetry:

- **Free Verse:** Classified as **"Rhythmic Prose."** While acknowledged as potentially "good writing" or "evocative," it is excluded from the category of Poetry because it lacks the "essential ingredients" of rhythm and rhyme.
    
- **Visual Lineation:** The act of using the `Enter` key to create artificial pauses (line breaks) is viewed as a **theatrical or visual formatting choice** rather than a poetic one. If a text reads like a standard sentence when written as a paragraph, it is categorized as prose.
    
- **Performance-Dependent Text:** Works that require a specific "actor’s delivery" (dramatic whispering, forced pausing) to feel "poetic" are excluded. The structure must exist in the text itself, not the performance.
    

## 4. Analogy of the Recorded Speech

A central argument in this definition is the **Speech-to-Music Analogy**:

1. **Music** = Organized sound with melody/rhythm.
    
2. **Speech** = Communication using language.
    
3. **Recording a Speech** = **Creating Music.**
    

Therefore, recording or writing down a highly emotional speech and adding line breaks does not transform the "prose" into "poetry"; it simply creates "formatted speech."
