# LEX-0010: TOURISM

**Domain:** Societas (The Inter-Relational Domain)
**Genus:** Geo-Relational Interaction

## Definition
The temporary displacement of an entity from its primary residence to a distant node for the consumption of experience, transaction, or novelty.

## Logical Constraints
- **Temporary:** Home return expected? Yes (If no → Migration)
- **Voluntary:** Freely chosen? Yes (If no → Displacement / Exile)
- **Non-Local:** Crosses effort threshold? Yes (If no → Recreation / Local Commute)

## Essential Components
- **Gravity (Pull):** The inherent desirability of the destination node that overcomes the friction of distance.
- **Effort (Gravity Debt):** The energy expended (time, currency, calories) to bridge the gap between home and destination.
- **Novelty:** The degree of difference (cultural, geographic, or systemic) between the origin node and the destination node.

## Exclusions
- **Not Migration:** Tourism requires the intent to return.
- **Not Commuting:** Commuting is functional and repetitive within a local grid; Tourism is experiential and crosses a distance floor.
- **Not Business Travel:** Pure Tourism is motivated by the destination’s "Gravity," whereas business travel is motivated by "Transaction."

## Architecture of Difference (The True Gravity Index)
The "True Gravity Index" (TGI) refines the measurement of Tourism by shifting focus from political "Border Crossings" (The Sovereignty Paradox) to "Effort and Pull."

### The Four Rules of the GMI (Global Magnet Index)
1. **The Non-Adjacent Requirement:** Visitors must originate from a region that does not share a land or bridge border. This removes "Neighbor Noise" (e.g., Hong Kong/Macau, Johor Bahru).
2. **The Distance Floor (3,000km):** Visitors are only ranked if they travel more than 3,000km. This filters out regional weekenders and highlights true "Pilgrimage" travel.
3. **The Isolated Entity Clause:** Geographically detached territories (e.g., Hawaii, Réunion, Tahiti) are treated as separate entities regardless of political passport.
4. **Weighted Distance (Passenger-Kilometers):** Instead of counting "heads," we count total kilometers. London to Sydney (17,000km) counts for ~50x more "Gravity" than London to Paris (340km).

### TGI Rankings (Magnet Cities)
Under this framework, the "Most Desired Cities" are those that exert the most powerful pull:
- **London / Paris / New York:** Remain high as they pull massive volume from all continents simultaneously.
- **Tokyo:** The highest-ranked city with a "Pure" arrival base (no land borders, high average distance).
- **Honolulu:** Jumps into the Top 10 globally due to the "Isolated Entity" recognition.
- **Dubai:** The ultimate "Transit Magnet" that successfully converts global long-haul transit into stays.
- **Sydney:** The "Point King," as almost 100% of non-NZ visitors fly extreme distances.

## Cosmograph Suggestion
**Visual Structure:** A series of concentric rings representing "Gravity Thresholds." The "Origin Node" sits at the center. Destinations are plotted based on their "Pull" (line thickness) and "Effort" (distance from center). The "True Gravity" of a city is visualized as its ability to distort the outer rings toward itself, showing how it "bends" the travel grid.
