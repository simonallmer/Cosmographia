# LEX-0018: HEGEMONY

**Domain:** Societas (The Social Domain)
**Genus:** Systemic Dominance

## Definition
The political, economic, or military predominance or control of one state or entity over others. Unlike direct empire, hegemony often operates through cultural influence and the setting of universal norms.

## Logical Constraints
- **Predominance:** Is one entity clearly leading? Yes (If no → Balance of Power)
- **Influence:** Does it shape the behavior of others? Yes (If no → Isolation)
- **Normalization:** Are its values seen as 'standard'? Yes (If no → Pure Coercion)

## Essential Components
- **Soft Power:** The ability to influence through culture and values.
- **Structural Power:** The ability to set the rules of the system (e.g., global trade).
- **Consensus:** The degree to which the dominated entities accept the hegemon's leadership.

## Architecture of Difference
- **vs. Imperialism:** Imperialism is direct rule; Hegemony is indirect leadership.
- **vs. Sovereignty:** Hegemony limits the practical sovereignty of secondary states.

## Examples
- The Pax Romana.
- The global influence of Hollywood and the US Dollar (American Hegemony).
- The metric system (Scientific Hegemony).

## Cosmograph Suggestion
**Visual Structure:** A large central star around which smaller satellites revolve. The orbit lines are thick and colored the same as the central star, suggesting a shared system of meaning.
