# LEX-0001: BOOK

**Domain:** Technica (The Artificial Domain)
**Genus:** Physical Information Carrier

## Definition
A physical, bound medium consisting of a sequence of pages containing recorded information, distinguished by a minimum volume of 49 pages and a permanent structural union.

## Logical Constraints
- **Materiality:** Is Tangible? Yes (If no → Electronic Book)
- **Unity:** Is Bound? Yes (If no → Manuscript / Folder)
- **Quantity:** Page Count ≥ 49 (If no → Pamphlet)
- **Frequency:** Periodic? No (If no → Magazine / Journal)

## Essential Components
- **Substrate:** The physical material (usually paper) that carries the information.
- **Binding:** The structural union that holds the pages together as a singular unit.
- **Text:** The recorded symbolic information within the vessel.

## Exclusions
- **Not an Electronic Book:** Lacks the physical substrate.
- **Not a Pamphlet:** Insufficient page count for weight/authority.
- **Not a Magazine:** Lacks the "permanent" non-periodic nature of a singular volume.

## Architecture of Difference (Comparisons)
- **vs. Codex:** A Book implies contemporary manufacturing; Codex refers to the historical ancestor.
- **vs. Scroll:** A Scroll is a continuous substrate; a Book is a sequenced substrate through pages.

## Examples
- The Gutenberg Bible (1450s)
- Hardcover Edition of '1984'
- The Diamond Sutra (Technically a Scroll by strict structural union standards)

## Cosmograph Suggestion
**Visual Structure:** A rectangular frame (the substrate) intersected by two heavy vertical lines on the left edge (the binding). Sub-lines fan out from the center-right to represent pages. A notation at the base reads `{ n ≥ 49 }`. This represents the threshold where a collection of paper becomes a "Book."
