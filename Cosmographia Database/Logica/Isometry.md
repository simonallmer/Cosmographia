# LEX-0025: ISOMETRY

**Domain:** Logica (The Foundational Domain)
**Genus:** Geometric Transformation

## Definition
A distance-preserving transformation between metric spaces. In an isometric transformation, the distance between any two points in the original space is equal to the distance between their corresponding points in the new space.

## Logical Constraints
- **Distance Preservation:** is $d(x,y) = d(f(x), f(y))$? Yes (If no → Homothety / Distortion)
- **Congruence:** Does the shape remain 'equal'? Yes (If no → Scaling / Stretching)
- **Bijectivity:** Is it a one-to-one mapping? Yes

## Essential Components
- **Translation:** Moving an object without rotating or resizing.
- **Rotation:** Turning an object around a fixed point.
- **Reflection:** Creating a mirror image of an object.

## Architecture of Difference
- **vs. Scaling:** Scaling changes distance; Isometry preserves it.
- **vs. Topology:** Topology allows stretching; Isometry forbids it.

## Examples
- Moving a chair across a room (Translation).
- Turning a steering wheel (Rotation).
- Looking in a flat mirror (Reflection).

## Cosmograph Suggestion
**Visual Structure:** Two identical hexagons side-by-side, but one is rotated 30 degrees. A series of parallel arrows connect the vertices of the two shapes, showing the transformation.
